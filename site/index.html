yepitran runs your mobile app on a real iOS Simulator and Android Emulator, follows a short script, and hands back a video, screenshots and a verdict you can trust. It never blames your app for a flaky device.

One verdict. No guessing.

Every attempt starts on a cleanly booted device. A failure counts against your app only when it happens again on a fresh one.

PASS

Every step passed on a real device. Share the video and screenshots with your reviewer.

APP FAIL

Your app did the wrong thing, twice, on two clean devices. The report shows exactly what was on screen.

INFRA FAIL

The device or tooling broke. This says nothing about your app, and the report says what yepitran tried.

A script anyone can read

One step per line, matched against what the user actually sees. Your coding agent can write it from a PR description.

  • Taps that landIt taps the part of a button that nothing else covers, so sheets and overlays don't steal the tap.
  • Typing that sticksText is read back after every entry, so autocorrect can't quietly change it.
  • Its own devicesyepitran uses only its own simulator and emulator, so it never touches another session's device.
# add-task.proof
launch
wait "Yep Todo"
tap "Add task"

tap "Save"
assert gone "New task"
assert visible "Try yepitran"
screenshot task-added

Proof on every pull request

The GitHub Action runs your scenario on GitHub's runners, uploads the video and screenshots, and can post the verdict on the PR. Free for public repos.

  • iOS on macOS runnersA fresh simulator for every run.
  • Android on Linux runnersA real emulator with hardware acceleration.
  • Fails the check only on a real failureThe step fails unless the verdict is PASS, and the report says which kind of failure it was.
- uses: codenameakshay/yepitran@v0
  with:
    platform: ios
    app: build/ios/iphonesimulator/Runner.app
    scenario: .yepitran/smoke.proof
    comment: true

Built to never flake on infrastructure

Simulators that hang, adb servers that die and emulators that crash are recovered, or reported plainly. We break it on purpose before every release.
